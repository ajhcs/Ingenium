# Art direction

Find the part of this subject that deserves an audience. Look for a relationship, a contradiction, a gesture, a peculiar phrase or a silence. Pick the one you would defend. Give it control of a consequential decision such as the sequence of pages, the material under the reader's hand, the way a control responds or what occupies the most space.

**Make the first encounter count.** Decide what a person should notice at a distance, then give them a reason to come closer. The second reading can reveal a consequence, a joke, a troubling detail or the information they need to act. A public instruction may deserve absolute clarity on the first reading. A book cover can afford to withhold something until it is picked up.

**Spend the work on the idea.** Draw the component, make the mark, set the type or prototype the gesture that carries the piece. Cut any framing copy that exists to announce how inventive the result is. If the explanation in the handoff does all the work, revise the artifact.

**Try the strongest rival.** It may deserve a rough sketch on the workbench. Compare what each lets the audience perceive and what each sacrifices. When asked to lead, deliver the stronger piece and own the judgment. When the person wants a comparison, show the smallest pair that makes the decision real. Keep internal alternatives off the delivery list when one finished artifact was requested.

**Put the idea under pressure.** Substitute a different subject, remove an expressive element or change a fact that should affect the form. Inspect what actually happens. In a technical figure, preserve meanings and conventions while experimenting with sequence or emphasis. In expressive work, discomfort or ambiguity can be the intention. Keep these as design judgments and describe any actual audience observations separately.

For an optional worked example, open [Common Measure](../assets/common-measure.html) in a browser. Two playing intervals determine an engraved score and a percussion duet. Change the bell from seven pulses to six while keeping the woodblock at five, then to five. Listen for the next shared strike and inspect the changing shape and lower notation. A circular score makes the return visible. The straight notation below it makes exact attacks easier to count. Save SVG to keep the current setting as editable text and paths. The HTML is the editable instrument. Skip this example when worked sources are withheld.
