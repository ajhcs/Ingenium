# Connected wireframes as iterative definition

Use this route for consequential interaction choices in an application, agent tool, engineering workbench or workflow. Keep Ingenium usable independently: model-specific obligations below apply when the task is model-backed. Small text corrections and settled component edits do not require a new study. Preserve the user's scope, authorization and withheld references.

## Ground a small connected path

Inspect relevant professional tools and actual UI precedents before drawing the interaction. Reuse already inspected evidence when it fits. Record the source, observed interface or behavior, proposed adaptation and limits; distinguish directly observed screenshots or interactions from documentation, located examples and routed findings. A source-access failure is a limit, not evidence of a feature. References inform choices rather than require feature parity or copied assets. Do not turn one study's app count into a universal quota.

Start promptly with one representative connected path that resolves a material uncertainty. Use authorized representative content and enough states to show the action and outcome. A field catalog alone is not a user interface; disconnected engineering specification cards do not demonstrate a workflow. Where they affect the selected interaction or context, show the workspace, navigation, selection, relevant tools and contextual information in the plain low-fidelity interface. Keep model/API annotations beside the interface rather than dominating it. Keep editable source and inspect actual rendered pixels at the intended size.

## Make behavior inspectable

Expose the relevant fields, options, selection sources, free-entry rules, defaults, unknowns, actions, state transitions and side effects. Show enough to distinguish before, local draft, applied change and persisted result where those boundaries exist. Explain validation, refusal, correction, cancel, undo and recovery within the selected path; label any unrendered or unexecuted route. Do not infer a successful save, restored state or complete workflow from an action label.

Where guidance, canvas or detailed views share information, state who owns the values and selection. Demonstrate the relevant linkage without inventing duplicate records or requiring duplicate entry. Layout and disclosure choices remain proposals until reviewed. An edit lock, forced sequence, commit gesture or save-before-navigation rule is not implied by a polished sketch or an existing implementation. Preserve free movement when requested; make unresolved tensions explicit.

Controls follow purpose and data semantics. A slider, dropdown, palette or drag gesture is an option, not a requirement because it was mentioned. Precise quantities need meaningful units, bounds and resolution. Stable references differ from copied labels; visual proximity, lines and frames do not establish connectivity, ownership or containment. A target-system diagram does not authorize operating that system.

For an interactive demo, exercise the actual controls, relevant keyboard/focus behavior, collapse/return, selection synchronization and resizing. Record the tested state, command or gesture, observed result and limits; screenshots alone do not prove those behaviors. For a static wireframe, report them as specified or untested. Deliver only the requested artifact types; a raster export does not replace editable source.

## Reconcile the model and the interface

For a model-backed task, identify the authoritative source and map displayed states and interactive elements through stable IDs or resolvable source anchors to their purpose, requirements, model properties, typed data/commands, constraints and acceptance procedures. Keep native supported behavior, new model/API proposals and unknown semantics distinct. Current API limitations qualify implementation evidence; they must not silently become target-product requirements.

Check consistency in both directions within a stated scope: each displayed action has a model purpose and binding, and each required in-scope action/state has a UI or justified external route. Check in-scope requirements and behaviors as well: each needs a realization and acceptance procedure, or an explicit gap. Matching every control does not establish requirement coverage. Include disabled/modal availability and distinguish proposed feedback from an executed validation receipt. Mark omissions, deferred workflows and unsupported persistence explicitly. Do not invent a widget for every internal field or require one screen per scenario step. Parser success, a projected graph or a trace matrix is bounded evidence, not proof of arbitrary semantics, behavior or engineering acceptance.

Review actual pixels and model/action coverage independently when warranted and available. Feed findings back into user needs, requirements, model semantics, data contracts, constraints and acceptance checks; then redraw and recheck affected material. Bind each check receipt to the exact authoritative model/source revision, sketch/view revision and input/provenance it inspected; an earlier pass cannot qualify edited artifacts. If definitions conflict, reconcile them rather than polishing over the discrepancy.

## Stop at the agreed review boundary

Show the first useful connected study before expanding into every workflow or control. Preserve the user's feedback gate and earlier versions. Approval of a visual kind does not settle every layout, behavior or product decision. Continue already authorized work without adding a new approval ritual; do not infer implementation authorization from a design review.

A concise handoff identifies the representative path, editable/rendered outputs, source/requirement bindings, assumptions and questions, observed versus planned checks, coverage limits and safest next review. A sketch remains a proposal until accepted; it does not establish implementation, full accessibility, conformance or engineering adequacy. Source changes, reviewed merge, packaged guidance and deployment into an installed client are separate outcomes. This guidance grants no publication, installation or production authority.
