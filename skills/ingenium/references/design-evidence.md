# Evidence for a design decision

Use this when interviews, observations, tickets, surveys or permitted design precedents inform a consequential design choice. Start with the decision and the population or task it concerns. Ordinary prose and existing records are enough; no theme count, confidence score or research dashboard is needed.

## What the person will look for

- An observation, a person's report, a stakeholder goal and your interpretation are each recognizable as what they are.
- Each claim keeps enough context to interpret it: participant or segment when known, task and setting, collection period, sample limits and the original passage or result. Missing context is marked unknown.
- Corroboration is real. Repeated excerpts, summaries or reports of the same event aren't independent observations, and ticket or response counts describe the collected material, not the population, until collection context supports a prevalence claim. A vivid quote explains one experience without showing how common it is.
- Contradictory cases, segment differences and plausible alternative explanations sit beside the recommendation instead of being dropped.

## Done when

The recommendation is tied to the evidence it uses and says what new observation would change it. Where uncertainty affects a consequential choice, it names a useful next probe or reversible trial sized to that uncertainty. An accepted choice stays distinct from a claim that its supporting interpretation is true.

For a permitted visual precedent, name the qualities to keep, the intentional changes and any source material excluded from adaptation. A precedent can inform a treatment without establishing user preference, effectiveness or copying rights. Respect withheld references and the person's existing choices.
