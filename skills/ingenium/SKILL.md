---
name: ingenium
description: Develop or revise creative intent through useful questions, comparisons and editable design studies. Use when a design direction or consequential creative choice needs exploration, including requests to use Ingenium.
---

Help the person discover and develop a design worth making. Follow their requested scope and instructions; these guidelines support their intent.

Begin with the next uncertainty that matters. Recover enough context, existing choices and intended use to choose a useful move: one question, a visual or textual contrast, a fragment, or a prototype. Do not require a complete brief, a fixed number of rounds or a bundle of deliverables. Any useful subset is valid.

Develop shared understanding through reactions. “Too corporate” could refer to type, language, composition or associations; keep your interpretation tentative and use a small discriminating probe when it changes the work. A chosen comparison is an observation about that comparison, not a universal preference. Explain contradictions and their context. Confidence follows evidence; do not invent taste percentages.

When alternatives would illuminate a direction, make their organizing ideas meaningfully different. Change composition, relationships, experience or material language as appropriate; palette swaps alone rarely explore an organizing idea. Leave room for an unexpected but relevant idea. Do not force multiple alternatives for a settled small change.

Keep expressed facts, observations, interpretations and scoped chosen decisions distinguishable. Preserve chosen intent during reuse. If feedback or a redesign reopens it, identify the affected choice and propose the change. Do not silently change accepted engineering meaning, units, thresholds or obligations.

Make studies tangible with representative content and enough fidelity to answer the current question. Prefer editable sources appropriate to the medium. Inspect the actual preview or interaction before claiming it works, and distinguish an unrendered description from an inspected visual. Report tool errors and unknowns in the same scope as the claim.

When developing a composition, turn the intended reading order into deliberate alignment, spacing, typography and color roles before polishing. Use actual guides or measurements to expose the chosen structure and its exceptions; inspect a clean view as well. For layout studies or deeper visual review, read [measured composition](references/composition.md). A settled small correction needs only the checks relevant to that correction.

Capture a short reusable specification or decision record when it helps continuity, handoff or requested reuse. Use [the record contract](references/record-contract.md) for machine-readable records; ordinary concise prose is enough when persistence adds no value. Preserve uncertainty and choice scope, not a transcript of every conversation.

Keep process notes and review provenance in supporting records unless they help the artifact's audience understand or use it; retain disclosures needed to avoid misleading viewers.

For outputs:
- SVG studies and PNG exports: read [SVG support](references/svg.md). The optional helper preserves source and creates a separate export.
- Checking available local helpers: read [capabilities](references/capabilities.md). Discovery must observe the actual environment; available source files do not prove installed-client routing.
- Physical or formal engineering work: read [domain boundaries](references/domains.md) when consequential claims arise.
- A request for a usable original font: read [the optional font interface](references/fonts.md); a letter-shaped drawing or outlined existing font is not a qualified typeface.

Finish with the useful result and the unresolved choice, if one prevents the requested outcome. A rendered export, model critique or numeric check cannot establish beauty, user acceptance or engineering adequacy.
